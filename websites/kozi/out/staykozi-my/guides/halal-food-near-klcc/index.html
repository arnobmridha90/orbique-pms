Eating halal near KLCC

Malaysia is one of the easiest places in the world to eat halal. The one thing to know is how to read the signs, and where to start near Vortex KLCC.

Checked 2026-10-24. Rules and prices change, so confirm on the official sites linked below.

Look for the JAKIM logo, not "pork-free"

Halal certification in Malaysia comes from JAKIM, the Department of Islamic Development. Certified restaurants display the JAKIM halal logo. A "pork-free" or "no pork" sign is not the same thing, and JAKIM has warned that such signs can mislead Muslim diners. When it matters to you, look for the logo at the counter.

Quick rule: JAKIM logo means certified halal. "Muslim-owned" or "Muslim-run" usually means halal food but not always certification. "Pork-free" alone is not halal.

Near KLCC

  • Suria KLCC, the mall under the Twin Towers. Ahh-Yum in the Signatures Food Court (Level 2) is listed as halal-certified. Serai's central kitchen is halal-certified. Melur and Thyme serves no alcohol, drinks included.
  • Damascus on Jalan Sultan Ismail, the same road as Vortex KLCC, is a Muslim-owned restaurant.
  • Mamak restaurants on Jalan Ampang are Indian-Muslim restaurants, open late and good for roti canai and teh tarik.

Kampung Baru for Malay food

Kampung Baru, across the river from KLCC, is the city's Malay village and a hub for Malay food such as nasi lemak. Many stalls take cash only, so carry small notes.

Jalan Alor

Jalan Alor's night market is famous, but most of its Chinese stalls are not halal. Check each stall before you order.
